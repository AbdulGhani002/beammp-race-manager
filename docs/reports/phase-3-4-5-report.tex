All three phases are on the server. This is what each one does, what changed
after your testing, and what the game itself will not allow.

Your build plan was also read back line by line against what was running. Five
things it listed for phases 1 to 5 had never been written: Team, experience and
levels, kick and ban and granting XP, records that split by class, and a
qualifying session that sets the grid. They are written now and they have a
section of their own near the end. One of them, Team, I had wrongly told you
was covered by the race lobby. It was not, and it is built properly now.

\section{Phase 3: the bottom bar}

The five buttons work. The server decides whether you may press one, holds the
car still for as long as the job takes, and adds the penalty. The game does the
work.

\begin{longtable}{@{}p{24mm}p{12mm}p{16mm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{70mm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Button} & \textbf{Hold} & \textbf{Penalty} & \textbf{What it does} \\
\midrule
\endhead
Reposition & 5s & 60s  & Back onto the course, facing the way it runs. Section 2. \\
Spare tire & 30s & 30s & A tire off the rack. Section 3. \\
Repair     & 60s & 30s & The whole car rebuilt, in place. \\
Fuel +25\% & 20s & none & A quarter of a tank, or a full one in the pit. \\
Re-rack    & 30s & none & Pit only. Fills the spare rack again. \\
Lights     & none & none & Headlights, fog, hazards, lightbar, siren, horn, flash. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

The penalty lands when the hold starts, not when it ends, so quitting half way
through buys nothing. A job the game cannot do gives the seconds back. Outside
a run everything is instant and free. Every number lives on the server and can
change without anyone reinstalling.

Each job makes a noise while the car is held, from the files you sent, and
only while a race is on. Spare tire is the impact driver six times, one per
nut. Fuel is the bottle at four fifths speed. Repair drills once and then
hammers and ratchets until the hold runs out, so it follows the hold rather
than a fixed count. Reposition and Re-rack are quiet, as you asked.

\section{Reposition}

Standing the car up where it rolled leaves it nose into whatever stopped it,
which is not what anyone means by the word. It goes back onto the leg you are
driving instead, the line from the gate you last passed to the one you are
going for, at the nearest point on that leg and pointing the way the course
runs.

Nearest is the fair one. No ground is given and none is taken, so it cannot be
used to skip a corner. The damage still comes with you. Outside a race it
stands the car up where it is, as before.

\section{The spare tire}

Your idea was the right one, and better than what was there before. Spare
tires are real parts bolted to the car, so the rack is read off the vehicle
rather than guessed at. A race truck carries two, a UTV one, plenty of things
none. That is the limit, not a number in a settings file, and a car with no
rack has no spare button at all.

Which road the button takes depends on how bad the tire is.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Flat with the wheel still under it: the air goes back in where you
        stopped and nothing else on the car is touched. This is most racing
        flats.
  \item Shredded, or the wheel is broken: the tires are replaced out of the
        parts list, the same job the vehicle selector does. It is the only
        thing the game can do for a tire that far gone.
\end{itemize}

Either way one comes off the rack. The button shows how many you have left and
greys out at zero. Re-rack is a pit job: it fills the rack again for the wait
and nothing on the clock.

One thing to know, because it decided the design. Replacing a part rebuilds
the whole car, not just the tires. So that road is only taken when the tire is
genuinely destroyed. Run it on every flat and each puncture hands out a free
repair, and nobody presses Repair again, since Repair takes twice as long for
the same penalty. The cost of doing it this way is that on an ordinary flat
the spares on the rack still look full on the model even though your count
went down. The count is what the rules run on.

\section{Phase 4: races you make and join}

The touchy motion start is gone. A race is a thing you make, and others join.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Pick a course, laps and mode, and make the race \textbf{public} or
        \textbf{invite only}, choosing people from the player list.
  \item Everyone who joins is placed on the grid. Nothing is armed. The start
        line does nothing, however many times it is crossed.
  \item The host presses \textbf{Start Race}. Only then does the line go live.
  \item Each driver's clock starts when they cross it, so the timing stays
        exact for everyone even if they roll off a second apart.
\end{enumerate}

Only the host can start it. A race that has started refuses late joiners. If
the host leaves before starting, the race passes to whoever joined first
rather than dying. One race per course, different courses at the same time are
fine, and solo practice works exactly as before.

\section{Phase 4: the pit and the speed zones}

A checkpoint can be marked as a \textbf{pit} when a course is built. It is not
part of the lap, it is drawn in its own colour, and while you are inside it:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Fuel fills to 100\% instead of a quarter.
  \item Repair and Spare tire add nothing to your race time.
  \item The holds still run in full, and the clock is still going. A stop
        costs the time it takes and nothing on top.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Speed zones} are the 37 mph idea, between any two checkpoints you
pick. A zone is a start gate, an end gate and a limit, and a course can have
as many as needed. The server reads the speeds it already collects, so
nothing is trusted to a driver's own game. Going over draws a warning first;
the 30 second penalty lands only after three seconds over, so one bump over a
crest is free, and it will not charge twice until you have dropped back under.

\section{Phase 5: the record books}

Every course keeps a book per mode. The Records button shows them.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Each driver's best corrected run, fastest first, with when it was set.
  \item The fastest clean lap anyone has driven, held separately.
  \item Your own position when you are outside the top ten.
  \item Controller and wheel are separate books, as agreed in phase 2.
\end{itemize}

The moment a race finishes, the run goes into the books before the results go
out, so the results screen hangs the badge on the row it belongs to:
\textbf{course record} or \textbf{personal best}. The books live on disk and
survive restarts.

What never makes the books: a run the server marked as suspect, because its
clock could not be vouched for, and any lap with a cut checkpoint in it,
which was already refused a best lap by the results.

\section{What the game will not allow}

Written down so nobody goes looking for a setting that does not exist.

\begin{itemize}
  \item One tire cannot be mended on its own. Air can go back in, which covers
        punctures. A destroyed one can only come back by rebuilding the car
        from its parts, which is why the spare works the way section 3
        describes.
  \item A car cannot be frozen for one player only in a way other players do
        not see. The hold freezes the car for everyone, which is correct for
        a race anyway.
  \item Gate widths come from the road the driving ai uses. Your dirt oval has
        one. A course built somewhere with no roads defined falls back to the
        width you set in the capture tool.
\end{itemize}

\section{The rest of what the plan promised}

Your build plan was read back line by line against what was on the server.
Five things it listed for phases 1 to 5 had not been written. They are written
now, and this is what each one does.

\subsection*{Team}

Invite somebody into your car, or ask to get into theirs. You both have to be
in the same vehicle model: it is checked when the offer goes out and again when
it is taken up, because cars change while an offer sits waiting. Swap to
another car afterwards and the team ends on its own and says why. So does one
of you leaving the server.

The results screen keeps both of your rows, because that is where the splits
and the penalties live, and puts a team standing beside them. Both of you have
to finish, and the team's time is your two corrected times added together, so a
team is only as quick as the pair of you.

\subsection*{Experience and levels}

Finishing pays by where you came: two hundred for the win, two less for each
place after it, never below twenty. That is your ladder, and the numbers are in
the settings file rather than the code, so they move without anyone
reinstalling. A level is a thousand points.

A run the clock could not vouch for pays nothing, the same rule the record
books use. Guests earn nothing either, having no record on disk to put it in.
XP sits beside the name, so it survives a restart for the same reason the name
does.

\subsection*{Kick, ban and granting XP}

Four commands in the console: \code{rm kick}, \code{rm ban}, \code{rm unban}
and \code{rm xp}. There is a \code{rm bans} to see who is on the list and why.

A ban follows the account rather than a name, which can be changed, or a
session, which is handed out fresh every join. It is on disk, so a restart does
not quietly let everybody back in. Somebody banned is turned away as they knock,
before anything is written down for them. In game nobody can act on somebody who
ranks as high as they do, or on themselves.

\subsection*{Records by class}

Your seventeen classes are in, as you wrote them, in your order, under the two
divisions you grouped them under. They are on the race screen as a row of
buttons under Limited and Unlimited.

A class is entered rather than worked out from the car. You allow every
vehicle, and two drivers in the same truck can be running different classes, so
the car cannot tell the server which book a run belongs in. The driver picks
one before they set off, or whoever makes the race picks one for the whole
grid, which is the way your bot announces them. Picking nothing is allowed: the
run still counts, it just sits outside the class books.

Your best is kept once per class, not once per course. A slower run in a second
class is that class's first entry rather than a run that failed to beat you.
Top of your own class earns a record of its own on the results screen, beside
the outright course record.

The record screen has a filter across the top: every class, or one of them. It
only offers the classes that have actually been raced on that course, so it
stays short.

One thing to check. Your list had a bullet reading Class 5/1600, 1600 and Class
9. I put that in as one class exactly as you wrote it. If those are meant to be
three separate books, say so and I will split them.

A class can also be pinned to particular vehicles later, which would refuse the
wrong car at the line. Nothing is pinned today, because you said every vehicle
is allowed for now.

\subsection*{Qualifying}

A course marked as qualifying now sets the grid. Whoever was quickest in the
qualifying session starts at the front of the next race on that course, and
anyone who was not in it lines up behind those who were, in the order they
joined, so a late arrival starts at the back rather than being turned away. The
order lasts the session: a grid earned this evening should not still be deciding
places next week.

\subsection*{One difference from the plan, named rather than hidden}

Speed zones were written as holding the car to the limit. What is built reads
your speed on the server, warns you, and charges thirty seconds if you stay over
for three. It does not physically slow the car. Enforcing by penalty cannot be
cheated by a driver's own game, which is why it went that way, but it is not the
same thing as a cap and you should know that.
